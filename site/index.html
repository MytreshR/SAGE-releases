Features

Details that decide whether it helps

Every one of these exists because the obvious version failed in a real conversation.

One question, one answer

A pause mid-sentence used to split a question into fragments, each answered on its own. SAGE stitches them back together, so it answers what you were actually asked.

It won't invent your CV

Answers are grounded in the resume you provide. Asked about something you have never touched, it says so and pivots to how you would approach it — rather than inventing a project that unravels under follow-up.

Invisible to screen sharing

The window is excluded from capture, so it does not appear in Zoom, Teams or Google Meet — and it stays out of the taskbar and Alt-Tab.

Hears your vocabulary

Your employers and technologies are fed to the recogniser, so Kubernetes, idempotency and your own company name come back spelled correctly instead of as near-homophones.

Screenshot solving

Capture a coding or system-design question on screen and get a worked solution with code and complexity — using a heavier model, because that answer is read, not spoken.

Not just interviews

General mode drops the candidate framing and helps in any live conversation — a meeting, a client call, a class.

Questions

Before you download

Is there a Mac or Linux version?

Not yet — version 1.0 is Windows 10/11 only. The app is built on Electron, so macOS and Linux builds are possible; they are not released because they have not been tested properly, and shipping an untested build of something you rely on mid-interview is worse than shipping nothing.

What does it cost to run?

SAGE is free to try for 13 minutes, then hours are $14.99 for three or $17.99 for five. That is the whole cost — transcription and answers run on our account, so there is no OpenAI bill on top and no API key to create. Nothing renews: when the hours run out you decide whether to buy more, and unused hours do not expire. The clock only runs while SAGE is listening, not while a session sits open.

Can the interviewer see it?

The window is excluded from screen capture, so it does not appear when you share your screen in Zoom, Teams or Google Meet, and it stays out of the taskbar and Alt-Tab. It cannot hide a second monitor, your eyes, or a proctoring tool that photographs your room — and some processes prohibit outside assistance entirely. Use it in line with the rules of whatever you are taking part in.

Where does my data go?

Audio is streamed to OpenAI for transcription and never stored by SAGE. Your API key is encrypted at rest on your own machine, and session logs stay in a local folder. No account, no server, no analytics — there is nowhere for it to be sent, because there is no backend.

Why does Windows warn me when I install it?

The installer is not code-signed yet, so Windows SmartScreen flags the publisher as unknown. Choose More info → Run anyway. A signing certificate is on the list; until then the warning is expected rather than a sign of anything wrong.

Does it answer everything it hears?

No. It filters small talk, filler, background noise and the stock phrases speech models invent during silence, and it only answers what looks like a genuine question. If a transcript is too garbled to be sure, it asks the interviewer to repeat rather than answering something that was never asked.
